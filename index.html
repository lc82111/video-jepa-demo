04 LDAD · retain action information

LDAD keeps action information in the change.

During training, LDAD asks a small decoder to recover the action from the latent displacement. Remove it before testing; CEM never uses it at test time.

07 Findings & boundaries

What the paper supports—and what it does not.

The results are encouraging, but not every combination is a clean win.

01RLD

Improves early transition learning.

RLD reaches 34% vs 16% Push-T success after the first pass and improves path straightness.

02LDAD

Makes the change action-aware.

5-step action ranking rises from 84% to 93%; the decoder is used only during training.

03HADP

Can change the chosen plan.

HADP changes 40.9% of CEM choices, and the point estimate is positive at both tested horizons.

04uncertainty

The long-horizon interval crosses zero.

The RLD + HADP aggregate CI is [−2, +10] pp: promising, not statistically established.

05transfer check

LDAD and HADP do not always stack.

The secondary transfer view improves at H100 but falls at H150, so the effect depends on the horizon.

06not validated

Continuous horizon interpolation was not tested.

The locked weights are λ=2 at H100 and λ=1 at H150; do not interpolate without new evaluation.

07scope

Current evidence uses one training seed.

Episode bootstrap intervals do not replace uncertainty over training seeds.
