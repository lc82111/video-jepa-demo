04 Action-Discriminative Representations (ADR)

07 Findings & boundaries

What the paper supports—and what it does not.

The results are encouraging, but not every combination is a clean win.

01RLT

Improves early transition learning.

RLT reaches 34% vs 16% Push-T success at epoch 1 and improves temporal straightness.

02ADR

Makes latent changes action-discriminative.

H5 action-ranking accuracy rises from 84% to 93%; the decoder is used only during training.

03HAP

Changes 40.9% of CEM decisions.

HAP lowers directional cost while keeping the learned model fixed; point estimates are positive at both tested horizons.

04uncertainty

The long-horizon interval crosses zero.

The RLT + HAP aggregate CI is [−2, +10] pp: promising, not statistically established.

05transfer check

ADR and HAP do not always stack.

The secondary transfer view improves at H=100 but falls at H=150, so the effect depends on the horizon.

06not validated

Continuous horizon interpolation was not tested.

The locked weights are λ=2 at H=100 and λ=1 at H=150; do not interpolate without new evaluation.

07scope

Current evidence uses one training seed.

Episode bootstrap intervals do not replace uncertainty over training seeds.
